## Part 3 — Government funding structure and mandate sweep

### How Government of Canada funding actually flows (explainer, not a new-org list)
Federal spending splits into **statutory** (fixed-formula, zero discretion — hard no for a
sponsorship ask) and **voted/discretionary** (Grants & Contributions programs, where officers have
real project-by-project latitude). ISED controls the largest single discretionary pool ($8.84B
planned 2026-27, but currently mid-cut). The **regional development agencies** (PacifiCan included)
are smaller in aggregate but far more locally relevant, and specifically run a **Regional
Innovation Ecosystems (RIE)** program — up to $3M, 50% cost-share, with **"business networking" and
"sector development" explicitly eligible**. This is the concrete mechanism to name when pitching
PacifiCan, not a generic sponsorship ask. No new org names surfaced from this angle.

### Federal mandate-based sweep (community/nonprofit or economic/innovation mandate)
- **Indigenous Services Canada — Aboriginal Entrepreneurship Program** — **Yes, evolved-VSW fit.** National program (non-repayable funding up to $250K for community businesses), distinct from the 3 local First Nations rows already flagged elsewhere for a governance-vs-econ-dev-arm question. Contact: ISC's Indigenous Business Navigator service (navigator@sac-isc.gc.ca), not a named individual.
- **Important non-candidate finding:** Sustainable Development Technology Canada (SDTC) was wound down (2024-25); its cleantech-startup funding mandate was folded into **NRC IRAP's new "Clean Technology" stream**. This isn't a new row — it means the **existing NRC IRAP row's story just got stronger** and should reflect the broadened mandate.
- Ruled out: ESDC social finance (fund-of-funds structure, no direct sponsor surface), Canadian Heritage (cultural infrastructure, not tech), Statistics Canada (data products, not a granting body), ECCC's Climate Action and Awareness Fund (weak — project funding, not event sponsorship), NRC-beyond-IRAP (fee-for-service R&D infrastructure), 3 of the 4 non-DIGITAL superclusters (Ocean/Protein Industries/Scale AI have no BC ties; NGen has some BC footprint but is a stretch fit even evolved).

### How BC funding actually flows (explainer, not a new-org list)
BC ministries **don't have a generic sponsorship slush fund** — money flows only through named,
published grant programs with fixed eligibility. The real test for any candidate: is there a
currently-open program whose eligibility doesn't explicitly exclude what VSW is. One ministry-level
finding did surface:

- **Ministry of Tourism, Arts, Culture and Sport — Destination Events Program** — up to $150K/event,
  real and discretionary. **Caveat:** its sibling BC Fairs, Festivals and Events Fund explicitly
  *excludes* "conventions, meetings and conferences" — worth checking Destination Events' own
  eligibility text carefully before treating this as a clean fit.

### BC mandate-based sweep
- **Creative BC** — **Yes.** Province-funded nonprofit, explicit "Interactive and Digital Media" mandate overlapping directly with VSW's tech/founder audience, already publicly tied to **Web Summit Vancouver 2026**. Contact needs verification (Robert Wong's listed focus is film tax credits, not interactive media — confirm before using).
- **BC Pavilion Corporation (PavCo)** — venue operator (BC Place, Vancouver Convention Centre), not a funder. Only relevant if VSW scales to convention-centre size — evolved-VSW-only, and a venue-partnership conversation rather than a sponsorship ask.
- Ruled out: Community Gaming Grants (wrong sector — no economic-development/tech category exists), DataBC/Developer Experience (internal-government-only), BC Arts Council (redundant with Creative BC), Ministry of Labour (no innovation angle), a provincial-level "Community Economic Development office" analogous to the City of Vancouver's (doesn't exist as a standalone unit).
