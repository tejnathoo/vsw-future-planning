# The "why us" sentence — before and after, 5 real orgs

**For Andrew. 2026-07-29.** Nothing here has been sent or written to the drafts doc. This is a
proposal for one change to the email template, shown against five organizations we have already
contacted, using their real sent copy as the "before."

---

## The change, in one line

**Replace this sentence:**

> *"From what we're seeing on the ground, those priorities line up strongly with the community
> that participates in our events each year."*

**With a sentence specific to the recipient, built from real audience data.**

This is a **swap, not an addition.** The email does not get longer, no other line moves, and the
paragraph keeps its existing shape. Andrew's structure, sentence count, and CTA are untouched.

### Why that particular sentence

It is the one line in the email that already tries to do this job. It asserts that our audience
matches their priorities and then offers nothing to support the assertion. It is also **byte-for-byte
identical across all twenty Tier 1 drafts**, which means it is the line most likely to read as a
mail merge, sitting immediately after the one line that was genuinely personalized.

Right now roughly 10% of the email varies by recipient, all of it inside one sentence about *them*.
Nothing in the message says what *they* get. Eight emails have gone out and none have been answered.

## 1. EY — Natalie Niedzialek, EY Entrepreneur Of The Year, Pacific
`Email A / chair@` · sent

**Before**

> We've been following your work on EY Entrepreneur Of The Year and your focus on celebrating the
> 130+ entrepreneurs shaping Canada's future through growth and innovation. *From what we're seeing
> on the ground, those priorities line up strongly with the community that participates in our
> events each year.*

**After**

> We've been following your work on EY Entrepreneur Of The Year and your focus on celebrating the
> 130+ entrepreneurs shaping Canada's future through growth and innovation. **The Pacific finalist
> pool and our attendee list are drawn from the same population: 696 BC organizations last year,
> and 45% of the people who listed a title were founders, CEOs, or owner-partners.**

*Why this one:* EOY is an awards program whose whole job is finding high-growth founders in the
region. Our strongest fact is that the region's founders are in one place once a year. The claim is
about overlap, which is verifiable, rather than about their need, which we would be guessing at.

## 2. National Bank of Canada — Krista Wilson, Director, Sponsorships & Donations
`Email B / community@` · sent

**Before**

> We've been following your work on the National Bank Investor Hub at Platform Calgary and your
> focus on backing early- and mid-stage companies driving innovation in financial services. *From
> what we're seeing on the ground, those priorities line up strongly with the community that
> participates in our events each year.*

**After**

> We've been following your work on the National Bank Investor Hub at Platform Calgary and your
> focus on backing early- and mid-stage companies driving innovation in financial services. **You
> have already built exactly this in Calgary. The equivalent Vancouver room gathers once a year:
> 696 organizations, 45% of titled attendees founders, CEOs, or owner-partners.**

*Why this one:* this is the strongest of the five, because it is not an argument, it is a
precedent. They have funded this shape of thing before, in another Canadian city, and we can point
at it. No dollar figures, per the wallet-sizing rule.

## 3. Version One Ventures — Boris Wertz, Founder & General Partner
`Email A / community@` · sent

**Before**

> We've been following your work on Fund V and your focus on backing exceptional, mission-driven
> founders creating new categories early. *From what we're seeing on the ground, those priorities
> line up strongly with the community that participates in our events each year.*

**After**

> We've been following your work on Fund V and your focus on backing exceptional, mission-driven
> founders creating new categories early. **Fund V backs founders earlier than most, and this is
> one of the few rooms in BC deliberately open to pre-idea and pre-seed builders, so you meet them
> before they are raising.**

*Why this one:* "early" is Version One's own stated thesis, and being open to pre-idea founders is
one of VSW's actual differentiators rather than something every event can claim. Boris is a
well-known local investor, so the register stays peer-level and short.

## 4. CVCA — Patrice Oliveira, Director, Development & Member Engagement
`Email A / community@` · sent to `info@cvca.ca`

**Before**

> We've been following your work on The 50 and your focus on fuelling the economy of the future by
> empowering the businesses of today. *From what we're seeing on the ground, those priorities line
> up strongly with the community that participates in our events each year.*

**After**

> We've been following your work on The 50 and your focus on fuelling the economy of the future by

*Why this one:* CVCA is an association, not a cheque-writer. Our own research says to frame this
around member value rather than sponsorship, and this sentence does that. It is the only one of the
five that sells to their members rather than to them.

## 5. Trade and Invest BC — Chris Heine, Senior Manager, Investor Services
`Email A / chair@` · sent

**Before**

> We've been following your investor services and the international trade missions you lead, as
> well as your focus on attracting investment into British Columbia and connecting BC's economy
> globally. *From what we're seeing on the ground, those priorities line up strongly with the
> community that participates in our events each year.*

**After**

> We've been following your work on the trade missions you lead and your focus on drawing
> investment into BC across cleantech, life sciences, and ICT. **Investor Services needs BC
> companies worth showing to inbound investors, and this is the one week a year that 696 of them

*Why this one:* also fixes a defect in the sent copy. The original initiative was *"your investor
services and the international trade missions you lead"* — two things joined by "and", which
collided with the sentence's own "and your focus on…" and forced the awkward "as well as" repair.
The tracker has since been corrected to "the trade missions you lead"; this draft never was.

## One open question for Andrew

Every one of these sentences describes the audience. None of them names what a partnership would
actually look like, because **there is no tier structure yet** — defining the format is what this
project is for.

The objection-handling guide already answers this well when a prospect asks directly: *"There's a
range, from hosting a workshop or session, to activating at an event, to straight sponsorship."*

**Should the email say that too?** The argument for: it converts a vague ask into a real menu, and
being early stops being a gap and starts being the offer, because a partner who engages now helps
shape what their involvement is instead of picking off a rate card. The argument against: it adds a
sentence, and Andrew has been cutting sentences, not adding them. Tej's call, then Andrew's.
