## Sam Lui — Growth Partner / Founder, Serious Otters
**Date posted:** ~May 2026 (1w before May 13)
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/samcllui_a-year-ago-at-vancouver-startup-week-right-activity-7456114478816866304-VVl0

**Post:**
> A year ago at Vancouver Startup Week, right after Anna Gradie's talk on fearless leadership, I made the decision to explore becoming a coach and soft launched my coaching practice.
>
> Of course, it feels perfectly fitting for me to share my update, and my next big step, after her presentation again this year.
>
> Here's what happened.
>
> Right after I decided to pursue coaching, instead of doing the standard research → study → certify → launch path, I asked myself how I could start practicing coaching right away. So I brought it into my work at Serious Otters. I added coaching elements into our consultations and just like that, I was practicing within the first week.
>
> As I kept going, I kept gathering data; and it's not just about the work, but also about myself. And after a while, something stood out as a major misalignment: I cannot stay detached enough to get out of the way of someone else's growth.
>
> But instead of seeing that as a weakness, I flipped it. I asked myself "If I can't stay detached, is there something that fits me even more than coaching?"
>
> Turns out, yes. I kept strengthening what worked and stripping away what didn't, and slowly I stopped trying to find where I fit and realized I was molding a path to me.
>
> What emerged was the role of a growth partner; my own blend of the most aligned parts of consulting and coaching. Not guiding from a distance, but being fully committed, accountable, and invested in a client's success over a long partnership. Using "caring too much" and "getting too attached" as strengths, not liabilities.
>
> That path is also what led to Ripple Amplifier, one of my proudest service offerings, and honestly one I feel the most aligned with.
>
> None of this would have happened if I didn't take that first step last year.
>
> Taking a bold step doesn't have to mean a life-altering commitment. More often, there are ways to take a series of small, reversible steps that get you closer and closer. The steps are not bold because of their size, they are bold because you are finally saying "F you" to the fears behind them. And if the path doesn't pan out, at least you can finally stop wondering and you can free yourself up to try the next thing.
>
> So, what's the next thing you've been wanting to try? And what's the smallest step you can take toward it today?

**Summary:** Sam traces a full year of career evolution back to a single VSW moment: Anna Gradie's talk on fearless leadership in 2025, which prompted him to soft-launch a coaching practice on the spot. What followed wasn't a straight line — he discovered coaching wasn't quite right, reframed his "flaw" of over-investment as a feature, and developed his own hybrid role (growth partner) and a service offering (Ripple Amplifier) that didn't exist before that decision. VSW here isn't backdrop — it's the recurring anchor: the place where the decision was made, and a year later, where the update gets shared.
