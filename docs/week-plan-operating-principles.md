# Week Plan — Operating Principles

Read this before editing [assets/artifact-src/week-plan.html](../assets/artifact-src/week-plan.html) or otherwise planning Tej's week. This is about *how the plan gets structured and committed to*, not about outreach content — see [docs/outreach-copy-playbook.md](outreach-copy-playbook.md) for that.

## What matters to Tej

1. **Underpromise, overdeliver.** Commit to what's realistically achievable, not what's aspirational. The plan should separate what's actually promised (the one goal) from what's a stretch or explicitly not promised — don't let stretch items quietly become commitments. When in doubt about a target, size it down and let the week beat it rather than miss it.
2. **Explicit task breakdown before committing to a plan.** A multi-phase piece of work doesn't go straight onto the plan as a vague line — it gets identified as an objective first, then broken down from that objective into the actual concrete tasks that deliver it. Do this decomposition before the week plan is written, not while it's being executed. One clear objective, decomposed into real tasks, is something to focus on and do — a vague multi-phase goal is not.

## The frameworks this structure is built on

The week plan is not a free-form to-do list — it's assembled against three specific frameworks (all summarized in Tej's Notion Knowledge base: [Deep Work](https://app.notion.com/p/Deep-Work-39a6b6f2b95b8121aa91f7002dcc235a), [Getting Things Done](https://app.notion.com/p/Getting-Things-Done-39a6b6f2b95b8178ac93d142f3ab5f0b), [The 4 Disciplines of Execution](https://app.notion.com/p/The-4-Disciplines-of-Execution-39a6b6f2b95b8188bbc2eb971491b5a6)). Work is not considered "structured" unless these are actually followed, not just referenced.

### 4 Disciplines of Execution (4DX) — the goal/scoreboard layer
- **One Wildly Important Goal (WIG)**, written "from X to Y by [when]," kept separate from the whirlwind (the ongoing operational load that must be sustained but isn't the thing being changed). This is the week plan's "the one goal this week" block.
- **Lead measures**, not just lag measures. The scoreboard should show the upstream, controllable numbers (drafts written, contacts sourced) alongside the outcome number (tier fully drafted), because lead measures are the steering wheel.
- **A players' scoreboard** — simple, visible, current, owned by the person doing the work. Stale numbers are worse than no scoreboard.
- **A weekly cadence**: commitments reviewed against last week's, scoreboard checked, next commitments made — this is the Friday close-the-week block.

### Getting Things Done (GTD) — the capture/review layer
- **Capture everything into one trusted place** so nothing depends on memory — this is why standing triggers and follow-up views exist instead of "remembering" to check things.
- **Every open item has a decided next physical action**, not a vague category sitting on a list.
- **The weekly review is the linchpin**: inboxes to zero, every tier confirmed to have a live next action, calendar checked both directions. This is non-negotiable, not optional — GTD's own claim is that when the system fails, it's almost always because the review stopped happening.
- Protect the calendar as the **hard landscape** — only things that truly must happen at a specific time go there.

### Deep Work — the execution layer
- Pick a deliberate scheduling model for deep blocks rather than hoping depth happens opportunistically. The week plan currently runs **rhythmic**: a protected deep block at roughly the same time most days.
- **Blocks are sized by output, not by the clock.** A block ends when its deliverable is hit; the time on the block is a guide, not a rule.
- **Shallow work gets a budget**, not unlimited access to the day — checkpoints and reactive buffers exist so admin/requests don't bleed into protected production time.
- **Close every day with a shutdown**: log actuals, confirm every open thread has a plan or is captured, set tomorrow's first task. This is what the wrap-up block in each day is for.

## How this should shape edits to the week plan

- Before adding anything to a day, ask: is this a decided next action (GTD), or is it still a vague objective that needs breaking down first? Don't put the vague version on the plan.
- Every number on the scoreboard should be a lead or lag measure that's actually true today, read from the live source — not a stale or aspirational figure.
- Don't let a "stretch" item creep into the main goal language. If it's not promised, it stays labeled as not promised.
- Estimates get corrected using real logged time, not re-guessed from scratch each week.
